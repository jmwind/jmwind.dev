Chapter 13

Do both

A CEO recently called me the "both" guy. Whenever he asks me to pick between two options, more often than not I say do both.

There's a lot of cargo culting around extreme focus and not doing too many things at once. "Put more wood behind fewer arrows"... but nobody said one arrow.

Imagine a tradeoff between going to the gym and having a great family life. Do both. You want to ship crazy quickly and be creative, but also build infrastructure? Why not both? You want to talk with your team with absolute clarity, or with more nuance? Do both.

The paradox mindset

This is the paradox mindset: accepting and engaging with competing demands. Demands that seem contradictory, are connected, and keep recurring. Removing one option today doesn't permanently remove the tension. So learn to live with them instead of eliminating one.

In a company, you have to set up the structures and culture to hold two very different types or styles of work. And you have to learn to do that earlier than you think.

I know CEOs are known for running after shiny projects and ideas, and teams often complain "we need to focus". That's also true. But it doesn't mean do one thing. Or be one kind of leader. Or that because you like ice cream, you can't have a six pack.

I'm not advocating for doing everything. You do have to practice subtraction. But if two ideas or skills seem important, do both.
