Chapter 10

Simmering procrastination

Being slow is often the most creative thing you can do.

Half of my work I get done very quickly. The other half I procrastinate on a lot. Is that just me? I'm happy to sit on it and let it simmer. Sometimes for a long time.

It drives my team nuts. Everything is important. We have mountains of work. But some things just don't feel right yet. You don't have the right information, the solution feels awkward, you need more research. The solution is boring.

In these cases I've learned, time and time again, that waiting is the most important thing you can do. Time gives you more context. Time gives you the creativity to try different approaches. And more often than you'd think, time makes the problem go away.

Be comfortable with simmering. Embrace it.

Active, not idle

Simmering procrastination is thinking about a problem without acting on it right away. It's active procrastination: you doodle, think, try things. But there's no rush to solve it yet. It's work in progress. You may have to let customers feel a bit of pain, but that pain can be less than shipping a shitty fix.

The hard part is the peer pressure to "just ship it". You're surrounded by task minions who want to check something off a list. But "just ship it" is often the enemy of creativity.

Immediate or never

The things I always do quickly: get back to people who are blocked or need a hand. If there's an annoying bug, I'll drop everything and fix it now. My MTTR (mean time to respond) is either immediate or never. There's no in between.

You should always be speeding up what's going too slowly and slowing down what's going too fast. I just can't tell you which is which.
