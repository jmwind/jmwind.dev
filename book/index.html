Book

You can't improve what you can't see.

I'm writing a book about leading teams that build things: the common patterns in technical teams, the nuances, and practical tools for the hard decisions that build great builders focused organizations and companies. It's not a book yet, some chapters are just napkin ideas short essays. I publish chapters as they come and will bundle them later. They're below. Send me a DM with feedback or a fist pump.

Contents

  1. The Problem Upgrade Chart

    We measure our self-worth with problems going away. But what if they don't?

  2. Platform Investments

    It's controversial, but I suggest you spend 50% of your r&d budget on your platform. Build a tech investment system that reinforces itself.

  3. Layerinitis

    Layerinitis is when teams put code where they are most comfortable while optimizing for speed vs putting the code where it belongs when considering a longer term perspective on the overall software system.

  4. Alignment > Autonomy

    It's easier to measure and feel high autonomy than it is to measure high alignment. But without alignment, autonomy is squandered.

  5. Going fast slowly

    Autonomy is great. But as you scale, the speed impact of only investing in getting fast fast will in fact slow you to a crawl.

  6. Problem solving vs problem planning

    Planning is important, but most planning is wasted time. Focus on building a problem solving culture and not a problem planning one.

  7. Product engineers ↗

    A guest chapter, written with Sherif Mansour.

  8. Lawnmower Managers

    Managing in this adult-accepting culture is a more rewarding job, and you can teach via the complex work that’s needed. You’ll have better managers for it.

  9. Time Horizon Friction

    You can cancel all your meetings, but they will come back if you don't see and squash your internal time horizon friction.

  10. Simmering procrastination

    Being slow is often the most creative thing you can do. Speed up what's going too slowly, slow down what's going too fast.

  11. Nuance vs clarity

    You can't lead by caveat. Think with nuance, then decide with clarity.

  12. I interviewed Mark Carney for a job

    Do your homework, and ask people to argue against their own position. Some people prepare a lifetime for problems they hope never arrive.

  13. Do both

    Put more wood behind fewer arrows. Nobody said one arrow. If two ideas or skills seem important, do both.
