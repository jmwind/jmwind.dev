Chapter 11

Nuance vs clarity

Lately I've seen people criticize founders for "lacking nuance." It's a fair point, but also a deeply nuanced accusation. You can't lead by caveat. You can only think with nuance, then decide with clarity. Nuance helps you understand the world; clarity helps you ship.

Most people confuse the two, but they're not the same muscle. Clarity turns ideas into motion. It's what gets people aligned and things built. Bring nuance into every meeting and you paralyze execution. Bring clarity into every discussion and you oversimplify reality.

As a Canadian, I wasn't built for this. My dad was a UN negotiator, and we're raised on polite qualifiers and diplomatic hedging. Clarity is the leadership muscle I've had to work on the most over the years. My coach once told me, "Communicating is like throwing a ball to someone. Most often they don't even catch it." Clarity improves the odds that they catch it.

The art is knowing when to embrace complexity and when to cut through it. Kill nuance in meetings: this doesn't work, pick one direction, we're shipping Friday. Bring nuance to strategy: why did this pattern emerge, what are we not seeing, who does this really serve.

Here's the uncomfortable truth. The best founders are terrible at nuance in public and brilliant at it in private. They simplify ruthlessly outward while thinking complexly inward.

Nuance is for thinking. Clarity is for moving.
